Clio Voice Assistant

Helps a person with a speech impairment hold a conversation. It combines unclear speech, a match of their voice's spectrogram, Indian Sign Language fingerspelling and face cues to suggest messages. When the patient confirms one, it's read aloud.

    Spectrogram matcher

    Each recording goes through these steps: 16 kHz audio → 25 ms Hamming frames every 10 ms → 512-point FFT → 40-band mel filterbank → log → 13 MFCCs plus their deltas → per-recording normalisation. Dynamic time warping then compares it with the patient's own enrolled recordings, which absorbs slow, uneven dysarthric timing. The enrolled recordings below are synthetic voices made in your browser. Their vowels drift toward a neutral "uh" sound, the way they do in dysarthria.

    What runs on this page

    • The full fusion ranker, ported from the prototype's Python backend. Emergency signs and a nod or shake to a yes/no question take a fast path; everything else goes through a weighted scorer over a 33-phrase English, Hindi and Hinglish phrasebook.
    • The spectrogram pipeline (FFT, mel filterbank, MFCCs, DTW), written in plain JavaScript.
    • Reply suggestions that appear as soon as the partner speaks, a one-tap confirm, and effort tracking.
    • Optional re-ranking by Claude, if you allow it. Without it the heuristic works fully offline.

    Only in the full app

    • Live microphone and camera. This page has no device access, so the controls above stand in for them.
    • ElevenLabs Scribe speech-to-text and ElevenLabs voices: designed, or cloned after the consent form is signed. Revoking consent deletes the voice and its cached audio.
    • MediaPipe hand and face landmarks, an ISL letter classifier (k-NN on 254 features) and nod/shake detection.
    • The SQLite store for the speech and audio caches, phrasebook, enrolled recordings, sign samples, consent records and audit log.
